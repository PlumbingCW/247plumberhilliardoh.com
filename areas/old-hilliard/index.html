Plumber in Old Hilliard, Hilliard, OH

Old Hilliard is the city's historic core, with its own street grid and mostly one and two story single family homes. Here is what the city's plan says about it, and what that does and does not tell you about your plumbing.

Call (614) 656-1470

If you own a house or a building in Old Hilliard, you probably have heard that the pipes in an old neighborhood must be bad. That is not something anyone can know from a street name. This page sticks to what the city has published, says plainly what is not known and then covers what a plumber can actually check at your address.

What the city says about Old Hilliard

The City of Hilliard's Old Hilliard Community Plan describes the area as the historic downtown, with the original street grid and the Franklin County Fairgrounds beside it. The streets it names include Main Street, Cemetery Road, Columbia Street, Norwich Street, Wayne Street and Scioto Darby Road.

The plan says existing residential areas are mostly one and two story single family homes, and it asks for the historic residential character along Norwich Street between Columbia Street and Cemetery Road to be kept. Zoning includes an Old Hilliard Residential District and an Old Hilliard Mixed Use district. The plan does not draw a hard boundary, so this page uses the name the way the plan does.

Why the mix of uses matters for plumbing

A house in this part of town can sit a few doors from a shop, a restaurant or a newer apartment building. The plan mentions Landmark Lofts on Cemetery Road as a recent mixed use residential project. That mix means a drain that serves one family in one block can sit close to a business with grease and heavy fixture use in the next.

If your main drain backs up more than once, ask a plumber to look at the whole line with a camera, not only the nearest fixture. A sewer camera inspection shows the pipe material, the slope and any roots or breaks.

How old is the plumbing, honestly?

The city's history page says Hilliard was platted on September 1, 1853 and incorporated in 1869, and that the connection to Columbus water and sewer service in the 1960s opened the city to growth. Those dates describe the town, not your house. Homes in an old neighborhood are often a mix of original work, later additions and repairs from several decades, so two neighbors can have very different pipes.

Water in Old Hilliard homes

Columbus supplies water to Hilliard properties and says it softens that water to an average of 120 parts per million, about 7 grains per gallon. That is moderately hard. Over years it leaves scale inside tank water heaters, on fixtures and in older small diameter lines. A yearly flush of the tank is the cheap defense, and a water softener is an option if scale is a recurring problem for you.

What the city's infrastructure plan does and does not say

The Old Hilliard plan lists water and wastewater capacity improvements as an action item, with a preliminary estimate of about ten million dollars based on the city's 2022 Utility Capacity Analysis. That is a planning figure for the area as a whole. It does not say which streets, which pipes or when.

If you see city utility work near your home, ask the city what it covers. Pipes inside your property line, and your own service line, remain the owner's responsibility to maintain.

Permits and registration

The City of Hilliard's Building Standards Division says plumbing work needs a permit. It also says contractors must be registered with the Building Department, and you can use Hilliard 311 to check. Franklin County Public Health also does plumbing inspection in the county, so ask the Building Standards Division which office inspects your job before work starts.

The city also runs a sanitary backflow program. If you own a business or a mixed use building on Main Street, call the city's Operations department at (614) 334-2367 to ask whether the program applies to your property.
