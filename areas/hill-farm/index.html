Plumber in Hill Farm, Hilliard, OH

Hill Farm is one of Hilliard's newest subdivisions, still being built out. New homes fail in different ways than old ones, so this page is about warranty questions, first year calls and what is known about the neighborhood.

Call (614) 656-1470

A new house is not trouble free, and a new neighborhood has a different set of plumbing questions than an old one. The pipes in a new house are not old, so the useful questions are who is responsible for what, what usually shows up in the first few years and when the plumbing needs a professional rather than a builder's call.

What the city and local reporting say about Hill Farm

The City of Hilliard says phase one of Hill Farm has 229 single family lots on 204 acres, was approved by Hilliard City Council in 2021 and was under construction when the city published its notice. Hill Farm II would add about 300 single family lots on 265 acres, with 186 acres of open space, near Elliott, Scioto Darby and Langton roads.

Local reporting says City Council voted 5 to 2 on March 24, 2025 to rezone the Hill Farm II land. On April 14, 2025 council barred any extension of Jeffrelyn Drive into the new development. The city also says construction cannot proceed until Hilliard receives sewer connections from Columbus. The builder named in local reporting is M/I Homes.

What this means for a plumbing call

Because phase one was approved only a few years ago, many Hill Farm homes may still be under a builder warranty. Warranty terms vary by builder and by component, and we do not know the terms of yours. Read the warranty before you pay for repairs, and ask whether a repair by an outside plumber changes your coverage. That is a general point about new homes, not a statement about any specific builder.

What usually goes wrong early in a new house

  • ✓A supply line connection under a sink or behind a toilet that was hand tight and has loosened
  • ✓A fill valve or flapper that does not seal, so the toilet runs
  • ✓A water heater set at a poor temperature or a pressure relief line that was never inspected
  • ✓A sump pump that was installed but never tested under load
  • ✓Drain lines with construction debris that blocks a trap or an aerator

Most of these are small repairs, and a faucet repair or toilet repair visit takes care of many. Others are worth a closer look. A sump pump that has never run is a risk during the first heavy rain, so test it by lifting the float and watching it start. If it does not, sump pump repair is a short job compared with a wet basement.

Water quality in a new house

Columbus softens its water to an average of 120 parts per million, about 7 grains per gallon, and it supplies Hilliard properties. New fixtures and a new water heater collect scale from the first day. If your new water heater came with a warranty, keeping up the yearly flush the manual recommends is usually part of keeping it valid. A water softener is an option once you have seen how your water behaves.

Permits and registration

The City of Hilliard's Building Standards Division says plumbing work needs a permit, and that contractors must be registered with the Building Department, which you can check through Hilliard 311. In a new subdivision, the builder has usually pulled permits for the original work. For a later change, such as a basement bathroom, ask the Building Standards Division what applies.

What we do not know

We do not know the construction dates of individual Hill Farm houses, the pipe materials used or the warranty terms in your contract. We have no list of Hill Farm jobs to point to and this page does not describe any. Status of Hill Farm II beyond April 2025 is not something we have verified, so check with the city for current plans.
