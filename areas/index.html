Hilliard, the home area

Hilliard is a city of about 39 thousand people in Franklin County. It began as Hilliard's Station on the Piqua and Indiana Railroad in the 1850s, and the city gained its status in 1960. Old Hilliard still centers on Hilliard's Station Park, the start of the seven-mile Heritage Trail, with the Early Television Museum next door. Houses near the old town square are among the oldest in the city, which makes pipe age a practical question there.

That is why a plumber working in Old Hilliard thinks about supply line age first, and a plumber working in a newer street thinks about water heaters and fixtures first.

Newer Hilliard neighborhoods grew in waves after the 1950s, with Columbus water and sewer connections arriving in the 1960s and the I-270 Outerbelt bringing a second wave in the 1980s.

Water supply is from Columbus, which says it softens to an average of 120 parts per million, about 7 grains per gallon. The city asks homeowners who suspect a sewer backup through a floor drain to call its Operations Department at (614) 334-2367.

Dublin: the neighboring city

Dublin borders Hilliard to the north and northeast. It has two ZIP codes, 43016 and 43017, and we take calls in both. The Dublin emergency plumber page explains the age mix of its housing and what that means for plumbing.

Inside Hilliard, three neighborhoods have their own pages: Old Hilliard, the historic core around Main Street, Hill Farm, where new homes are still going up, and Hoffman Farms off Scioto Darby Road. Each page separates what the city has published from what is not known.

How the area affects the plumbing

Hilliard and Dublin share one water supplier, the City of Columbus, so the water arriving at the tap is the same moderately hard supply in both. That is why scale in tank water heaters, aerators and shower heads is a common find in either city.

Housing age is what separates them. Parts of Old Hilliard hold houses built well before the 1960s sewer connection, while much of the rest of Hilliard and Dublin was built in the decades after. A plumber arriving in Old Hilliard is more likely to find original supply lines and older drain pipe.

Basements matter too. Many homes in both cities have basements, and plenty have sump pits.

A pump that stopped or cannot keep up in a storm is a common seasonal call, which is why the sump pump page is worth reading before the first storm. Frozen and burst lines follow the first hard freeze, usually on exterior walls and in unheated garages, which is when the burst pipe repair page earns its place.

Business customers

Along Cemetery Road and the I-270 corridor, commercial calls look different from home calls. A restaurant grease line, an office restroom or a floor drain in a shop needs a plan that keeps the business open. The commercial plumbing page covers after-hours calls and how isolation of a leak is handled first.

Code references for this work

In both cities the same plumbing rules apply to the work itself, since Ohio's plumbing code is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). What can differ is who issues the permit, so we check that for your address.
