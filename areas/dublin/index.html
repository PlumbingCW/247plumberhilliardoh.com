Housing first: what Dublin's age mix means for your plumbing

Dublin began as a settlement along the Scioto River around 1803, and John Sells platted the village in 1810. Many original buildings in Historic Dublin are on the National Register of Historic Places. For most residents, that history matters less than what came after. The city says Dublin changed in the 1970s from a rural village into a suburban business center, helped by the Muirfield Village Golf Club and residential community, and it became a city in August 1987.

In practice, much of Dublin's housing is newer than Hilliard's older core. Homes from the 1980s through the 2000s now have water heaters near the end of their life, supply fittings that have aged and sump pumps that have run through many storms. Some homes built in the late 1970s through the mid-1990s may contain polybutylene supply pipe, which has a record of failing at fittings, so a leak call in that age range deserves a pipe-material check.

Water and the utility: what to know in Dublin

Columbus lists Dublin among the communities its Department of Public Utilities serves with water, sewer or both, and Columbus reports that it softens its water to an average of 120 parts per million, about 7 grains per gallon. That is moderately hard water. Scale builds up in tank heaters, so if yours is more than ten years old, a flush and an anode check are worth doing before it fails. Ask the utility for the figure at your address.
