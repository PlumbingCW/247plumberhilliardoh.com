Plumber in Hoffman Farms, Hilliard, OH

Hoffman Farms is a Hilliard subdivision off Scioto Darby Road, next to the new Hill Farm II land. This page covers what is documented about it and how to work out your own home's plumbing age.

Most of what makes a house a plumbing risk is its age, its water and how it has been used, not its subdivision name. For Hoffman Farms, the age is the part we cannot verify, so the most useful thing this page can do is show you how to find out for your own house.

What is documented about Hoffman Farms

The City of Hilliard's comprehensive plan names the Hoffman Farms subdivision when it recommends safer crossings of Scioto Darby Road to reach the municipal park. The Hilliard City Schools attendance list places the Estates at Hoffman Farms along Scioto Darby Road between Cosgray Road and Leppert Road.

Local reporting from 2025 says the Hill Farm II land touches the Estates at Hoffman Farms along Elliott Road, and calls Jeffrelyn Drive a stub street in Hoffman Farms. On April 14, 2025 Hilliard City Council barred any extension of Jeffrelyn Drive into Hill Farm II.

Find your own home's plumbing age

The city's comprehensive plan, using Census survey data, says nearly 60 percent of Hilliard's housing units were built since 1990. That is a citywide figure, not a Hoffman Farms figure. For your house, the Franklin County Auditor's property record lists the year built. Once you have it, you can estimate where your plumbing sits in its life.

These ranges are general plumbing guidance and not a statement about Hoffman Farms. A tank water heater often lasts roughly 8 to 12 years depending on the water and the maintenance. If yours is past ten years, a leak is a matter of when, and a planned water heater replacement costs less in damage than an emergency one.

Water in Hoffman Farms

Columbus supplies Hilliard properties and says it softens its water to an average of 120 parts per million, about 7 grains per gallon. That is moderately hard and builds scale inside heaters and on fixtures. Ask the utility for the figure at your address, and consider a water softener if scale shows up on faucets and showerheads.

Permits and registration

The City of Hilliard's Building Standards Division says plumbing work needs a permit, and that contractors must be registered with the Building Department, which you can check through Hilliard 311. For something like a water heater swap or a new bathroom, ask the division what applies before the work begins.
