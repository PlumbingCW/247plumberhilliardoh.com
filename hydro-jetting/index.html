Backups that keep coming back: when jetting is the next step

A cable punches a hole through a clog. Jetting uses water at high pressure, delivered through a nozzle that sprays forward and backward, to scour the whole inside of the pipe. The debris is washed downstream or pulled back out. It reaches the grease, scale and soft roots that a cable leaves behind.

  • ✓Grease buildup in kitchen and restaurant lines
  • ✓Heavy scale in older cast iron lines
  • ✓Soft root growth after a cable has opened the line
  • ✓Repeated clogs in the same line within a few months
  • ✓Sludge in long horizontal runs

How a jetting visit works

The plumber starts with a camera, because jetting is for sound pipe. If the camera shows a cracked, offset or collapsed section, jetting can make it worse, and the better step is repair. If the pipe is sound, the nozzle goes in through the cleanout. The pressure and flow are set for the pipe size and material.

After jetting, a second camera pass confirms the pipe wall is clean. You see the before and after footage and decide what comes next.

Why Hilliard homes use jetting

The city says its housing boom ran through the 1950s to the 1980s. Houses from that period often have cast iron drain lines. Cast iron roughens as it ages, and rough walls hold onto grease and paper. Jetting removes that layer, which is why a line that clogged every few months can run clean for a year or more afterward.

Commercial kitchens along busy corridors such as Cemetery Road face the same pattern in a harsher form. Grease from fryers and dishwashers coats the line, and jetting is part of the normal maintenance for a restaurant. See commercial plumbing for scheduled service.

Jetting and older pipes

A line that is thin or already cracked can be damaged by pressure. That is why we inspect first and explain what we see. If the camera shows damage, we say so and do not jet.
