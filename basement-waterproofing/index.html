What the City of Hilliard says

Hilliard runs a Sanitary Backflow Program for homeowners with repeated sewer backups during heavy rain. The city says the program does not apply to wet basements caused by water entering through foundations or other sources, and that this water typically comes from faulty sump pumps or problems with existing downspouts and drainage. That is a useful starting list for an assessment.

If you think water came in through a floor drain, the city asks you to call its Operations Department at (614) 334-2367 so the main line can be inspected. That visit costs you nothing to request, and it tells you whether the water is sewage.

Interior drain, exterior work and where we stop

Interior drain systems collect water at the footing and move it to a sump. Exterior waterproofing means excavating, sealing and installing drain tile around the foundation. Cracks in the wall or structural movement are a foundation and masonry problem. We handle the plumbing and drainage side, and we will tell you when a foundation specialist is the right call.

Old basements in older neighborhoods

In Old Hilliard and the 1950s subdivisions, basements may have older drain tile, floor drains tied directly to the sewer and sump pits that were added later. Ask us to check where each drain goes. A floor drain tied to the sanitary sewer is the one that can bring sewage back.

Code references for this work

Sump discharge, floor drains and backwater protection are covered by Ohio's plumbing code, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). Anything that touches a pump's wiring follows the National Electrical Code (NEC).
