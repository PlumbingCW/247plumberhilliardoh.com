Where the main line clogs

The main sewer line carries everything from your house to the public sewer. Blockages tend to form at the same places: where branch lines join the main, at a bend or a belly, at a joint that has shifted and at the transition between old and new pipe. Grease, wipes and roots collect there first.

The cleanout is a capped pipe, usually near the foundation or in the basement, and it gives us direct access. If you have never located yours, ask the plumber to show you on the visit. Knowing the cleanout is the single most useful bit of plumbing knowledge a homeowner can have.

How a main line gets cleaned

A powered cable with a cutting head runs through the line and breaks up the blockage. Roots are cut back to the pipe wall, grease is scraped and paper is broken down. Afterward we run a camera to confirm the line is open and look for the damage that caused the clog.

Heavy grease or scale calls for hydro jetting after the cable. Damage that the camera finds becomes a conversation about sewer line repair.

Homes in Hilliard that clog more often

Hilliard's residential growth ran from the 1950s through the 1980s, and the city says the connection to Columbus water and sewer in the 1960s opened the area for development. Many homes from that period have cast iron or clay laterals. Cast iron gets rough inside as it scales, which catches grease and paper. Clay joints invite roots.

If your house is in that range and the main line has clogged more than once, a camera inspection is better value than another cable visit. The footage shows whether cleaning will keep working or whether the line has a defect that needs repair.

What not to flush

Wipes marketed as flushable do not break down like paper. They tangle with roots and grease and are a leading cause of main line blockages. Paper, human waste and water are the only things a toilet should carry.
