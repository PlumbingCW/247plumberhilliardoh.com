Emergency Leak Detection in Hilliard, OH

A spinning meter, a damp ceiling or a bill that doubled all mean water is going somewhere. We find it first, then tell you what the fix costs.

Call (614) 656-1470

An active leak is an emergency when water is coming through a ceiling, spreading across a floor or running with nobody home. Call the 24/7 line, shut off the main water valve if you can reach it, and move belongings away from the wet area. If the ceiling is bulging, stay out from under it, because a bulge can drop plaster and water at once.

Start with the water meter

Shut off every tap and appliance that uses water, then look at the meter. If the small dial or flow indicator still moves, water is leaking somewhere between the meter and your fixtures. If the meter is still, the leak is likely downstream of a fixture that opens only when used, or it is a drip that needs a longer test.

Columbus is replacing meters at some Hilliard properties as part of its Enhanced Meter Project, which began in mid-June 2026. Most meters are indoors, installation is free and water can be shut off for a short time. After a swap, a ten-minute check of the connections and the dial is a good habit. Higher bills after a new meter do not always mean a leak, but a moving dial with everything off always does.

Pinpointing the leak before cutting anything

Opening a wall to look is the most expensive way to find a leak. A plumber can narrow it with a pressure test that isolates each line, acoustic listening gear that picks up the sound of escaping water through a surface, a moisture meter on the suspect wall and thermal imaging for warm lines. The goal is one small opening in the right place.

  • ✓Pressure test to learn which line is losing water
  • ✓Acoustic listening on the floor, wall or exposed pipe
  • ✓Moisture and thermal readings to confirm the wet area
  • ✓A short written finding that you can show your insurer

Slab leaks and under-floor leaks

Water under concrete announces itself with a warm patch, a constant hiss or a bill that creeps up with no obvious reason. Hot lines in a slab can be rerouted overhead rather than broken out, which is often cheaper than opening the floor. Ask for both options, since the right answer depends on access and the age of the piping.

Wet ceilings and walls: when a leak is an emergency

A ceiling stain under a bathroom usually traces to a shower valve, a drain connection or a supply line above. A stain below a kitchen can mean the same, or a dishwasher or ice maker line. In basements and crawlspaces, check for drips on joists and rust at old joints, since a slow leak can run for months.

What your insurer will want

Take photos before cleanup and keep any damaged materials until the adjuster has seen them. Many policies cover sudden damage but not the cost of finding or repairing a long-running leak, so ask early rather than assuming.
