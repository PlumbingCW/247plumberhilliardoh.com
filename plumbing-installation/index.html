Rough-in: the part you do not see

In a remodel, the plumbing goes in before drywall. The rough-in sets the drain slope, the vent connection and the supply stub-outs at the exact heights that the finished fixtures require. A mistake here means opening the wall later. We confirm fixture models and dimensions before cutting.

Basement bathrooms and the ejector pump

A basement drain that sits below the main sewer line cannot flow by gravity. A sewage ejector pump in a sealed basin lifts waste up to the main drain. It must be vented and fitted with a check valve, and it is a part you should have inspected every few years.

PEX, copper and PVC

Most new supply lines today are PEX or copper, and drains are PVC or ABS depending on code and location. We use the material that suits the job and the inspection requirements. In older Hilliard houses, where galvanized or cast iron remain, we often connect new work to the existing pipe with proper transitions.

Permits

The City of Hilliard states that plumbing work needs a permit and that contractors must be registered with the Building Department. Homeowners can do their own work on their own residence, but permits and inspections still apply. We handle the permit for the work we do and tell you what inspections follow.

Laundry rooms, outdoor spigots and utility work

Laundry hookups need a standpipe or a washer box at the right height, a drain that can handle the discharge and shutoff valves you can reach. Outdoor frost-free hose bibs reach through the wall so the valve sits inside the heated space, which matters in an Ohio winter. We also install utility sinks, set up condensate drains for furnaces and water heaters and connect floor drains. Each of these is a small job on its own, and bundling them into one visit saves a trip.
