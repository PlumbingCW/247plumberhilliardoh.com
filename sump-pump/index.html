Emergency basement water: what a failing pump looks like

  • ✓The pit is full and the pump is silent
  • ✓The pump runs constantly but water level does not drop
  • ✓The pump cycles on and off rapidly
  • ✓A loud grinding or rattling from the pit
  • ✓The discharge line is dripping or the check valve clatters

A sump pump is only needed when it counts, which is during storms. That is also when a pump with a stuck float or a dead motor fails, so a pump that has gone years without testing is a risk.

How a sump system works

Groundwater or drain tile water flows to a pit set into the basement floor. A float or sensor starts the pump when the water rises and the pump sends it out through a discharge line and a check valve that keeps water from flowing back. The system fails at the weakest of these parts, and the cause is rarely the motor alone.

What the city says about basement water in Hilliard

Hilliard runs a Sanitary Backflow Program for homeowners who have had repeated sewer backups into their homes during heavy rain. The program does not cover wet basements caused by water entering through foundations or other sources, which the city says is typically caused by faulty sump pumps or problems with downspouts and drainage.

That tells you where to look first. If water comes up through a floor drain, it is a sanitary sewer issue, and the city asks you to call its Operations Department at (614) 334-2367 so the main line can be checked. If water seeps through walls or the slab, the sump, downspouts and grading are the places to start.

Backup pumps and power failure

The usual storm brings a power loss. A battery backup pump sits beside the main pump and runs when the power is out or the main pump fails. It is the most direct protection for a finished basement, and we size it for the inflow rather than the nameplate rating.

Discharge matters

Sump water should discharge outside, away from the foundation, and not into the sanitary sewer. If you are unsure where yours goes, a plumber can trace it on the visit.

Code references for this work

A sump pump circuit follows the National Electrical Code (NEC), and the discharge and check valve follow the International Plumbing Code (IPC) that Ohio's plumbing code is based on.
