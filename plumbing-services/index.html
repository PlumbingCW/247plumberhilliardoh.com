Emergency plumbing services

Start here if something is wrong right now. Most Hilliard emergencies begin with a clogged drain, a failed water heater or a burst pipe. Each page explains what to shut off first and what the visit involves.

How to pick the right page

Match the page to the symptom, not to the part you think failed. A ceiling stain could be a supply leak, a drain leak or a roof problem, so the leak detection page is the better start than a repair page.

A toilet that gurgles when the shower drains is usually a drain line problem, which belongs with sewer line cleaning. Lukewarm water from a tank heater could be a failed element, a tripped thermostat or a tank near the end of its life, and the water heater repair page lists how to tell them apart.

What changes the price

Nobody can price a hidden problem over the phone, and anyone who does is guessing. The plumber sets the price after finding the cause. Four things move it most: how easy the pipe or fixture is to reach, the age and material of the existing piping, whether parts are on the truck and whether the visit falls at night or on a holiday. You get the number before work starts, and you decide whether to go ahead.

Planned versus urgent: how the queue works

Urgent calls go first, because water that keeps moving does more damage every hour. Planned work is scheduled around you. If you are not sure which group you are in, describe what you see to the dispatcher. A slow drain can wait a day. A drain that is pushing water back up a floor drain cannot.

Safety first, on every page

  • ✓If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 from outside
  • ✓If water is near outlets, a panel or a light fixture, stay clear and cut power only from a dry spot
  • ✓If sewage is backing up, stop using every drain and toilet in the house
  • ✓If a pipe has burst, close the main shutoff and open a low faucet to drain the lines

Code references for this work

Every job listed here is done to the plumbing code in force in Ohio, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), with the National Electrical Code (NEC) governing any power connection.

Planned work, and how it is scheduled

Planned work is booked around your schedule. Call the same number and tell the dispatcher what you want done. Water heater installs, repiping, fixture installs and sewer camera inspections are the most common planned jobs.

The city of Hilliard states that plumbing work needs a permit, and that every contractor in the city must be registered with its Building Department, so we confirm the permit requirement when you schedule.

Not every plumbing job belongs in this list. If a job is a structural foundation repair, a gas appliance conversion that needs a different trade or a project that needs engineered plans, we will tell you and point you toward the right contractor instead of taking the call and stalling.
