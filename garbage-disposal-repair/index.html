A leaking garbage disposal is an emergency under the sink, because water may be reaching the cabinet floor and anything stored there. Switch the disposal off at the wall switch, close the shutoff valves under the sink and call our 24/7 line. Never put a hand in the grinding chamber, even when the unit is off at the switch. Use tongs or pliers for anything stuck inside.

Humming, jammed or leaking: which one do you have?

The sound the disposal makes tells you what failed. A hum with no spin means the flywheel is jammed. Silence points to a tripped reset button, a dead outlet or a failed motor. A grinding or rattling noise means a foreign object, such as a bottle cap or a piece of silverware. Water under the sink means a seal, a drain connection or the disposal casing has failed.

Repair or replace the unit

A disposal that leaks from the casing cannot be repaired and has to be replaced. A unit that just jammed can often be freed in minutes. If the motor failed and the disposal is more than eight or ten years old, buying a new one costs about the same as a motor repair would, and you get a new warranty.

What goes down a disposal and what does not

  • ✓Fine to grind: small food scraps with cold water running
  • ✓Avoid: grease, oil and fat that coat the line downstream
  • ✓Avoid: coffee grounds in volume, pasta and rice that swell
  • ✓Avoid: fibrous foods like celery or corn husks in large amounts
  • ✓Never: glass, metal, bones that are large, or any chemical cleaner

A disposal does not cause a slow kitchen drain on its own, but it puts more solid waste into a line that may already be coated with grease. If your kitchen sink backs up even with the disposal clear, see drain cleaning.

Installation and dishwasher connections

When we install a new disposal, we check the sink flange, set the mounting ring, connect the drain and the dishwasher inlet, and test for leaks under pressure and under running water. A common cause of a leak after an owner-install is a loose dishwasher knockout plug or a poorly seated drain gasket, both easy to check.

Electrical safety

A disposal runs on household power, usually on a switched outlet under the sink. If a unit sparks, smells burnt or trips the breaker repeatedly, switch it off at the panel and do not reset it again until it has been inspected.

Code references for this work

A disposal's power connection follows the National Electrical Code (NEC), and its drain connection follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC) that Ohio's plumbing code is based on.
