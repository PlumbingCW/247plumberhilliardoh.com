Sewage backup emergency: signs it is the sewer line, not a clog

A clog in one fixture is a local problem. A sewer line problem shows up in several places at once, and it returns even after cabling.

  • ✓Gurgling or bubbling in more than one fixture
  • ✓Sewage smell in the yard or basement
  • ✓Backups that return days after a cleaning
  • ✓Wet or sunken spots in the lawn over the line
  • ✓Slow drains throughout the house at the same time

Why a camera comes first

A sewer line cannot be diagnosed by guessing. A camera on a push cable travels through the cleanout and shows what is wrong: roots entering a joint, a cracked or collapsed section, a belly where water pools, grease buildup or an offset joint. It also measures the distance from the cleanout, so the repair is planned for that point rather than along the whole yard.

The footage is yours to keep. If you are buying or selling a home, a sewer camera inspection is the way to know what you are getting, and it costs far less than a surprise repair after closing.

Lining and bursting limit how much of the yard has to be opened up. Not every line qualifies, which is why the camera finding decides the plan. After the repair we run the camera again to confirm the line flows clean.

Older homes and cast iron in Hilliard

Hilliard connected to the Columbus regional sewer system in the 1960s, and the 1960s through 1980s homes built around it often have cast iron or clay laterals that are now more than 40 years old. Cast iron scales from the inside and eventually cracks. Clay joints let roots in. Both are the kind of problem a camera sees quickly.

Who owns which stretch of pipe matters before any digging. Responsibility depends on where the break is, so ask the Hilliard Building Standards Division or the sewer utility before you assume the repair is yours, and ask what permit applies.

Permits and registered contractors

The city's Building Standards Division states that plumbing work needs a permit, and every contractor working in Hilliard must be registered with the Building Department. Ask for the registration before you sign, and use Hilliard 311 to verify it.
