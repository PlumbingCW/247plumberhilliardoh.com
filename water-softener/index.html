What Columbus reports about its water

Columbus, which supplies Hilliard properties, states that it softens its water to an average of 120 parts per million, or about 7 grains per gallon. That is moderately hard, and it explains the white scale on fixtures and glassware, the crust on shower heads and the sediment in water heater tanks. What a softener does for your plumbing

Scale narrows pipes, coats heating elements and shortens appliance life. Softening reduces it. It can help a water heater and a tankless unit last longer, though neither replaces a periodic flush. It does not stop corrosion of old steel pipe.

Certification and honest claims

Look for softeners tested to NSF/ANSI Standard 44 and filters tested to the standard that matches the claim, such as NSF/ANSI 53 for contaminant reduction or 58 for reverse osmosis. We do not make health claims about drinking water. For that, ask your physician or the water utility.

Salt, water use and upkeep

A salt-based softener regenerates on a schedule and uses salt and some water each cycle. The brine tank needs refilling every few weeks, and bridged salt that forms a crust should be broken up. Wash-down and iron-removal products can extend resin life when the water has iron in it, and the water report tells you whether that applies. Keep the bypass valve accessible, and write the date of the last service on the unit so the next visit starts from a record.
