Plumbing in a railroad town that kept growing

Hilliard began as Hilliard's Station, a stop on the Piqua and Indiana Railroad that John Hilliard platted in 1853. The village incorporated in 1869 with 280 residents. If your house sits near Hilliard's Station Park or along Main Street in Old Hilliard, it may be among the oldest in town, and older houses are where a plumber looks first for worn supply lines and aging drain pipe.

The big wave came later. The city says three large subdivisions arrived in the 1950s, the connection to Columbus water and sewer opened the 1960s to development, and the completion of the I-270 Outerbelt sparked a second wave in the 1980s.

That means a lot of Hilliard homes are now 40 to 70 years old. At that age, fixtures, shutoff valves and water heaters are the parts that fail, so a plumber who knows the era can often predict what a leak call will turn out to be.

Hard water, meters and permits: three local facts that matter

Columbus, which supplies Hilliard properties, says it softens its water to an average of 120 parts per million, about 7 grains per gallon. Moderately hard water leaves mineral scale, and scale builds up inside tank water heaters. If yours is past ten years old and rumbles when it heats, sediment is a likely reason, which is why a water heater repair visit often starts with a flush and an inspection of the anode rod.

Columbus is also replacing meters at some Hilliard properties under its Enhanced Meter Project, beginning in mid-June 2026. Most meters are indoors, installation is free and water can be shut off briefly during the swap. After any meter work, glance at the connections for drips and watch the meter dial with every tap closed, because a spinning dial means water is going somewhere. That test is the first step of leak detection.

The third fact is about paperwork. The city's Building Standards Division says plumbing work needs a permit and that every contractor working in Hilliard must be registered with the Building Department. Ask for the registration before you agree to a repair, and use Hilliard 311 to check it.
