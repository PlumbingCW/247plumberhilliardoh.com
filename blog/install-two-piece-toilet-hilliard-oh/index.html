How to Install a Two-Piece Toilet in Hilliard, OH

How a two-piece toilet is installed, from removing the old one to setting the wax ring, with the checks that prevent a leak and the cases that need a plumber.

Call (614) 656-1470

By the Hilliard 24/7 Plumbing team · Published 2026-11-14 · Updated 2026-11-14

A two-piece toilet has a separate tank and bowl. Replacing one is a job many homeowners manage in an afternoon. The steps are simple, but the places where it goes wrong are specific: a poorly seated wax ring, uneven tightening or a flange that is damaged. This guide covers the order of work and the traps.

Before you begin

  • ✓A new toilet, a wax ring or a wax free seal, closet bolts and a new supply line
  • ✓A wrench, a putty knife, a bucket, old towels and rubber gloves
  • ✓A helper, since a toilet is heavy and awkward to carry

Measure the rough-in distance, which is the gap from the wall to the center of the floor flange. The most common is twelve inches. A toilet built for a different rough-in will not sit right. Measure before you buy.

Removing the old toilet

Turn off the shutoff valve, flush and hold the handle to drain the tank. Sponge out the rest of the water. Disconnect the supply line, remove the tank bolts and lift off the tank. Then pry off the bolt caps, remove the nuts at the base and lift the bowl straight up. Set it on a towel or in a bucket.

Stuff a rag in the drain opening to keep sewer gas from rising and to keep tools from dropping in. Scrape off the old wax ring with a putty knife. Check the flange: it should be solid, level with or slightly above the finished floor and free of cracks.

The flange tells you whether to go ahead

Flange condition and the next move
ConditionWhat to do
Solid and level with the floorProceed with a new wax ring
Cracked or brokenUse a repair ring or call a plumber
Below the floor surfaceUse a thicker ring or a spacer
Rotted subfloor around itStop and call a plumber

A bad flange or a soft subfloor is a repair, not an install. If the floor around the toilet is spongy, the subfloor has taken in water for some time, and the cause may be a past leak. A plumber can find and fix the source, which the toilet repair page explains.

Setting the new bowl

Place new closet bolts in the flange slots. Set the wax ring on the flange, or on the bottom of the bowl, as the manufacturer directs. Lower the bowl straight down onto the bolts, press down firmly and rock it only slightly. Do not lift it again once it is seated, because the seal will break and you will need a new ring.

Thread the nuts by hand and tighten them evenly, a little at a time on each side, alternating. Overtightening cracks the porcelain. Stop when the bowl does not rock and the base sits flat.

The tank and the supply

Fit the rubber gasket on the tank's flush valve, place the tank on the bowl and tighten the tank bolts evenly. The tank should sit level. Connect the supply line, open the valve slowly and let the tank fill. Flush several times and check for leaks around the base, the tank bolts and the supply connection.

Checks after installing

  • ✓No water around the base after three flushes
  • ✓The bowl does not rock when you push on it
  • ✓The tank is level and the lid sits flat
  • ✓The water level in the tank stops at the marked line
  • ✓A dry paper towel at the supply connection stays dry

Choosing a toilet

Pick a bowl shape that fits the space. Elongated bowls are more comfortable and need a bit more room than round ones. Check the clearance from the wall to the front of any vanity or door. Look at the flush system too. A gravity flush is simple and quiet to repair. A pressure-assisted one is louder and has parts that cost more.

Keep the box and the manual. The manual lists the parts, the water level line and the fill valve type. If you ever need a repair, it will save time.

Caulk, bolts and the finishing touches

After the toilet is set and tested, trim the closet bolts with a hacksaw and fit the caps. Run a thin bead of caulk around the base if you want a clean line, but leave a small gap at the back so that a leak at the seal can show on the floor instead of being hidden. Some plumbers skip caulk for this reason.

Wipe the bowl and tank, put the seat on and adjust the water level in the tank to the line marked inside. Dispose of the old toilet properly. A porcelain toilet is heavy, and many transfer stations take it. Keep the receipt for the new one and the manual. If anything leaks in the first month, that paper is what you need.

Check the floor around the base once a week for a month. A joint that is going to weep usually shows itself in that time, and catching it early protects the subfloor.

A Hilliard note: older homes and floor flanges

Many Hilliard homes date from the decades after the 1950s, when the city grew with the connection to Columbus water and sewer. In houses that old, a toilet flange may have been set when the floor was thinner or have corroded cast iron under the bowl. Check the flange closely before you buy a new seal.

The Building Standards Division states that plumbing work needs a permit, and a like-for-like toilet swap is usually minor, but check with the division when you are unsure.

When to call a plumber

Call if the flange is damaged, the floor is soft, the toilet keeps leaking at the base or you are moving the toilet to a new spot. Moving one means changing the drain, and that needs a permit and a vent check. The plumbing installation page covers a planned fixture job.
