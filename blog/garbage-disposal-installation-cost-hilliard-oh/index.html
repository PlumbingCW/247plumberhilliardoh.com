Garbage Disposal Installation Cost in Hilliard, OH

What decides the price of a new garbage disposal in Hilliard, which parts of the job are easy and where a plumber saves you from a leak.

Call (614) 656-1470

By the Hilliard 24/7 Plumbing team · Published 2026-11-14 · Updated 2026-11-14

The price of a disposal installation is set by four things: the unit you buy, whether a disposal was already there, what the sink drain looks like underneath and whether the electrical side is ready. A straight swap of one unit for a similar one is the cheapest job. A first-time install in a sink that never had one is the most involved. Everything else falls between.

The short answer on cost

A swap takes the least time because the mounting, the drain connection and the power cord are already in place. A first install needs a new drain opening, a dishwasher connection and a power source. Quotes vary because those extras vary, so a price given without seeing the sink is a guess. A plumber should look under the cabinet first, then give you one number before starting.

This page does not print dollar figures. Prices change with parts and with the hour of the call, and a printed number would be wrong for your sink. What it can do is show you what is inside a quote, so you can compare two quotes line by line.

Four things that move the price

What changes a disposal quote
FactorLower cost whenHigher cost when
Unit sizeA basic motor fits a small householdA larger motor and sound insulation are chosen
Existing setupA disposal is there and the flange fitsThe sink has no opening or the mount is cracked
Drain layoutThe trap lines up with the new outletThe pipes need a new tailpiece or a trap rebuild
PowerA switched outlet is already under the sinkWiring or an outlet must be added

Motor size is the part most buyers think about, and it matters least to the final bill. A mid-sized motor handles normal household scraps. A bigger one makes sense when the household cooks a lot, because it jams less. The part that surprises people is the labor when the old drain lines do not match the new outlet.

What a plumber does that a quick swap skips

  • ✓Checks that the sink flange is sound and sealed with plumber's putty
  • ✓Aligns the drain outlet so water flows downhill into the trap
  • ✓Connects the dishwasher drain and knocks out the plug, which is the step most often forgotten
  • ✓Tests for leaks with the water running, then again after ten minutes

The forgotten plug in the dishwasher inlet is worth a paragraph of its own. A new disposal has a knockout plug inside the dishwasher inlet. If it stays in, the dishwasher cannot drain, and water backs into the dishwasher on the first cycle. Removing it takes a screwdriver and a hammer. It is also the first thing an inspector, or a plumber returning on a call, looks for.

Power and safety come first

A disposal needs power, and the safe way to work is to turn the breaker off and test the cord or wires before touching anything. If the sink has no switched outlet, the electrical part is a job for an electrician. Do not run a disposal from an extension cord or leave exposed wiring under a wet cabinet. If you are not sure about any step, stop and call a plumber.

Never put a hand into a disposal, even when it is off at the switch, without cutting power at the breaker. Use pliers or the hex key on the bottom of the unit to free a jam. Those tools are what the manufacturer intends for the job.

Can you do it yourself?

A swap is within reach of a careful homeowner with basic tools. The sink flange, the mounting ring and the drain connection come in the box with clear steps. The risk is a slow leak under the cabinet, which will not show for days. If you do the job, run water for ten minutes, wipe every joint dry and check again the next morning.

A first install is a different job. It means cutting into the drain, possibly adding an outlet and connecting the dishwasher line. If you are unsure, the plumbing installation team handles it as a planned job, and the garbage disposal repair page covers units that are jammed, humming or leaking.

Signs that you should repair, not replace

A jam or a tripped reset button is usually a repair. A unit that hums and does not turn has a stuck impeller, and a hex key often frees it. A leak from the bottom, the side seam or the housing is a replacement. Metal fatigue and worn seals cannot be repaired, and the next failure is a puddle in the cabinet.

Age matters too. A disposal that has run for more than a decade is closer to the end of its life than the start. If the unit is old and you are paying for labor anyway, replacing it often costs less over the next few years than repairing it.

A Hilliard note: the sewer and what goes down the sink

Hilliard homes drain into the Columbus sewer system, and the city of Hilliard runs a Sanitary Backflow Program for homes in older sewer areas. If your floor drain has ever backed up, treat the disposal as a convenience and not as a way to dispose of grease, rice, coffee grounds or fibrous peelings. These clog a line downstream of the disposal, where you cannot reach them.

If a kitchen line backs up after a disposal install, the cause is usually something that was already in the pipe. The drain cleaning page explains how a plumber confirms that.

What to ask before you accept a quote

Questions that make two quotes comparable

  • ✓Is the dishwasher connection included

  • ✓Is a power outlet or switch part of the job or not
  • ✓What is covered if a joint leaks in the first week

Code references for this work

A disposal needs a wiring method that follows the National Electrical Code (NEC) and a drain connection that follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC), the model code behind Ohio's plumbing code.
