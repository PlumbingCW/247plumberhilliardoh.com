How to Replace a Water Heater Heating Element in Hilliard, OH

How to tell a failed heating element from a failed thermostat, how to test one safely and why elements in a tank wear out faster than you expect.

Call (614) 656-1470

By the Hilliard 24/7 Plumbing team · Published 2026-11-14 · Updated 2026-11-14

An electric water heater makes heat with two heating elements. When one fails, the water goes lukewarm or runs out fast, and many owners assume the whole tank is dead. Often it is a single part. Learning to tell the difference can save a heater, and knowing when to stop can save you from a shock.

Symptoms of a failed element

  • ✓Hot water that runs out far sooner than it used to
  • ✓Lukewarm water that never gets fully hot
  • ✓No hot water at all, with the breaker on
  • ✓A breaker that trips whenever the heater runs

An element that is failed on the lower side gives you a small amount of hot water, then lukewarm. A failed upper element can leave you with no hot water, because the upper element heats first. A shorted element can trip the breaker.

Power off, then test

Switch off the breaker, check it with a non-contact voltage tester and remove the access panel. Disconnect the wires from the element. Set a multimeter to resistance, touch the probes to the two screw terminals on the element and read the number. A working element reads a low resistance. A reading of open or zero means it is bad.

Also touch one probe to a terminal and the other to the metal tank. A good element does not conduct to the tank. If it does, the element has a short to ground and must be replaced.

Reading the test
ReadingMeaning
Low steady resistance across the terminalsElement is likely fine
Open circuit, no readingElement has burned through
Reading from terminal to tankShort to ground, replace
Normal reading but no heatCheck the thermostat

Replacing an element

The tank must be drained below the element before it is unscrewed. Attach a hose to the drain valve, run it outdoors or to a floor drain and open a hot tap upstairs so the tank empties. Use an element wrench to unscrew the old element, fit a new gasket and new element of the same wattage and voltage, then close the drain and refill.

Refill the tank before turning the power back on, and open a hot tap until water runs steadily. If you turn the power on while the tank is not full, the new element burns out in seconds. This is the most common way a replacement element fails.

Why elements wear out

Elements fail because of scale, sediment and dry firing. In hard water, mineral crust builds on the surface, traps heat and makes the metal run hotter than designed. The lower element sits in the sediment, and it can end up buried. Over time the sheath cracks and the element shorts.

Ways to extend element life

  • ✓Flush the tank once a year to remove sediment
  • ✓Check the anode rod every few years
  • ✓Keep the thermostat at a moderate setting
  • ✓Never run the heater with the tank partly empty
  • ✓Replace both elements together when one fails on an old tank

A tank that is more than ten years old and has a failed element deserves a hard look. If the tank is rusted, leaking or noisy, a new element is a short-term fix. The water heater repair page lists how a plumber decides, and the water heater replacement page covers the next step.

Choosing the replacement part

Elements come in different wattages and shapes. Screw-in types are common, and some tanks use a flange with four bolts. Low density elements run cooler and are less prone to scale, which helps in hard water. Check the label on your heater and bring the old element to the store, so you can match the thread, wattage and voltage.

A new element is only half the repair. Flush the tank while it is drained, since sediment on the bottom will bury the lower element again. A plumber often does both during the same visit.

A few safety rules worth repeating

Never work on a heater with the power on, even for a quick look. Confirm the circuit is dead with a tester every time, because a breaker can be mislabeled or a second circuit can feed the same unit. Keep the wiring dry, and do not stand in water. Wear glasses when you open the access panel, and gloves when you handle the insulation.

If the heater is in a finished space, check that there is a drain pan and a pipe from the relief valve to a safe spot. Water that escapes from a tank can do a lot of damage. Look at the relief pipe for signs of dripping. A drip from it means pressure or temperature is too high, and it deserves attention from a plumber before it becomes a bigger problem.

Finally, know your limit. A repair that takes a few minutes for a professional can take an afternoon for a beginner, and a mistake costs more than the part. When in doubt, call.

A Hilliard note: hard water is the usual culprit

Columbus supplies Hilliard properties with water that it softens to an average of 120 parts per million, about 7 grains per gallon. Moderately hard water leaves scale on every heating surface. In homes without a softener, a lower element that fails at the eight or ten year mark is common, and the rumbling noise of a tank often comes from sediment on the bottom.

Flushing the tank is a plumber's standard first step on an older heater for exactly this reason.

When to call instead of doing it yourself

If the wiring looks burnt, if the drain valve will not open, if you do not own a meter or if the tank is leaking, stop. Water and electricity share this appliance, and a mistake can hurt. A plumber carries the right parts and can usually finish in one visit.
