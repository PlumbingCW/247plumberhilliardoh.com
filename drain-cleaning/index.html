When a clogged drain becomes an emergency

A single slow sink is a scheduled job. Several fixtures gurgling at once, a basement floor drain bubbling, or a toilet that backs up when the shower runs all point to the main drain, and that cannot wait for a business-day slot. Same-day and after-hours calls are what the 24/7 line is for.

  • ✓Water or sewage coming up in a tub, shower or floor drain
  • ✓More than one fixture draining slowly or gurgling
  • ✓A toilet that bubbles when another fixture drains
  • ✓A kitchen sink that backs up into the dishwasher side

Which kind of clog do you have

The location of the symptoms tells a plumber where the clog is. One fixture means a local clog in its trap or branch line, usually hair in a bathroom or grease and food in a kitchen. Two or more fixtures on the same floor mean a branch line is blocked. Every fixture at once, or a basement drain that backs up first, means the main line is blocked.

Snaking, cabling and when jetting is the better tool

A drain snake or cable breaks through a clog and restores flow. It is fast, and it is the right first move for hair, paper and most household blockages. It cuts a hole through grease and roots rather than removing them, so the drain can narrow again within weeks.

When a line clogs again after cabling, or the cable stops short of the cleanout, we recommend hydro jetting to scour the pipe wall and a camera look through the line afterward. If the camera shows a crack or a belly in the pipe, the conversation moves to sewer line repair.

Kitchen sink and tub clogs in Hilliard homes

Houses built in the 1950s through the 1980s, which covers much of Hilliard, often have older cast iron or galvanized drain branches that have rough interior walls. Grease catches on that roughness and builds up layer by layer. If your kitchen drain slows every few months, the pipe is telling you something that a bottle of cleaner will not fix.

Tubs and showers clog with hair and soap scum at the first bend below the drain. A plumber can often pull that mass out by hand in a few minutes, without cabling the whole line. Ask about it when you call, since it affects what the visit should cost.

What to do while you wait

Run a flashlight down the nearest cleanout if you know where it is. Tell the dispatcher whether it is wet or dry, since a dry cleanout usually means the clog is downstream of it and a wet one means it is upstream. That one detail helps the plumber arrive with the right tools.

Code references for this work

Drains are designed to the venting and slope rules in the International Plumbing Code (IPC), which Ohio's plumbing code follows. A clog that keeps returning can be a slope or vent fault, not just debris.
