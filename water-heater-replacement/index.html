A failed water heater can release the whole tank onto the floor, which is a lot of water in a basement. If the tank is leaking, close the cold water valve above it, cut power at the breaker or turn the gas control to off, and call our 24/7 line. If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility first. A dispatcher will help you with the next steps.

Emergency replacement: how to tell the tank is finished

  • ✓Water pooling under the tank, not from a pipe or valve
  • ✓Rust-colored water from only the hot tap
  • ✓A tank more than ten to twelve years old with repeated repairs
  • ✓Popping or rumbling that returns right after a flush
  • ✓A scorched or sooty flue on a gas unit

Once a steel tank rusts through, there is no patching it. If your heater is leaking from the body, the quickest route to hot water is a replacement, and the visit starts with confirming that the water is coming from the tank.

What replacement involves

The plumber shuts off and drains the old heater, disconnects the water lines, venting and fuel or power, removes the unit and brings in the new one. The new tank is set in a drain pan where the space allows, connected with new flexible lines and valves, vented and tested. Fuel connections are leak-tested and the relief valve and its discharge pipe are checked.

Hard water, anodes and the life of the new tank

Columbus reports that it softens water to an average of 120 parts per million, about 7 grains per gallon. That is moderately hard, and it forms scale that coats heating elements and settles at the bottom of tanks. A new tank lasts longer if it is flushed once a year and the anode rod is checked every few years. We explain that on the visit and leave you with the schedule.

Some customers pair a new heater with a water softener. That is a choice, not a requirement, and water softener and filtration explains what it does and does not do for a heater.

Permits, code and what we handle

The city of Hilliard states that plumbing work needs a permit, and that every contractor working in the city must be registered with the Building Department. We tell you on the visit how the permit is handled for your address, and we leave the old unit hauled away. Ask whether the quote includes disposal, a new relief valve and an expansion tank where one is needed.

Insurance and a flooded basement

Sudden tank failure is often covered, though the cost of the heater itself usually is not. Photograph the leak before cleanup, keep the old tank until your adjuster says otherwise and ask your insurer about water damage restoration.

Code references for this work

A replacement tank is installed with a relief valve and discharge pipe as the International Plumbing Code (IPC) requires, and Ohio's plumbing code is based on it. Electric units follow the National Electrical Code (NEC).
